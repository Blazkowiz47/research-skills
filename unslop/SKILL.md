# Unslop

Improve clarity with the smallest useful edit. Keep text unchanged when it already
works. Do not rewrite merely to make it appear less machine-generated.

## Protect meaning first

For every genre, preserve facts, claim strength, uncertainty, scope, citations,
quotations, numbers, units, equations, identifiers, and precise technical terms.
Words such as vector, primitive, modality, or interface may have exact domain
meanings. Replace decorative jargon only when the substitute means the same thing.
Do not change code, literal strings, commands, or quoted material during prose cleanup.
Flag unsupported facts or missing sources instead of inventing replacements.

Follow the user's tone, authorship, venue, and formatting conventions. Use first
person only when it fits the author and genre. Never add opinions, emotional color,
deliberate mess, or personal experience to make the prose sound human.

For research reports, proposals, reviews, rebuttals, and scientific interpretations,
read [research-writing.md](references/research-writing.md). Its evidence and
reproducibility protections take priority over stylistic preferences.

## Edit

1. Identify the reader, purpose, and content that must survive the edit.
2. Remove filler, promotional claims, vague attributions, stock transitions, and
   repeated conclusions. Name the mechanism, observation, or consequence.
3. Prefer plain words and active verbs where they clarify the actor. Keep passive
   voice when the procedure or object matters more than the actor.
4. Split sentences that require backtracking. Preserve natural rhythm and the
   author's reasoning; do not force a fixed sentence length or list size.
5. State points directly. Avoid contrast formulas, artificial ranges, synonym
   cycling, excessive boldface, decorative headings, and conversational padding.
6. Use punctuation for meaning. Prefer sentences over chains of dashes; retain
   parentheses for citations, units, abbreviations, and necessary qualifications.
7. Compare the revision with the source. Restore lost uncertainty, qualifications,
   evidence, terminology, and reproducibility details.

Return the requested text. Explain changes only when useful or requested. If a
factual issue prevents an accurate revision, identify that issue separately without
silently strengthening or repairing the claim.
